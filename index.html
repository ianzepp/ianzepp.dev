The main story · Aug 2025—present

Learn the domain. Verify the output.

Since August 2025 I have worked full time on AI-assisted systems: a programming language and its Rust compiler, a multi-agent runtime and fleet orchestration layer, a multi-tenant platform, MCP security tooling, and the evaluation harnesses that keep the rest honest. I had no programming-language-theory background when the compiler started. I chose a domain where bluffing fails—a type system works or it doesn’t.

The process came first. For months I ran it by hand — goals and invariants before code, work in bounded phases, completion audited apart from implementation — until the coordination load outgrew one person. Then I built the tooling to hand dispatch and tactical judgment to agent fleets, keeping the problem definition and the audit for myself.

Agents coordinate by passing durable message handles, not by shipping full context between sessions. Routing models by task shape cut blended cost roughly 92% from early-2026 peaks. The volume numbers below are real and dated; they are not the thesis.

The operating method

How the factory runs.

The loop below ran by hand for months before any of it was automated. Agents now supply throughput and tactical dispatch; problem definition and audit stay human.

IGoal

Write the goal first.

The intended end state and its invariants go into a document that outlives the conversation. So far: 216 goal documents, 43 campaign plans, 91 ledgers.

IIPhases

Split into bounded phases.

Parallel agents get non-overlapping scopes. Read-only scouts go ahead of architecture changes; worktrees isolate anything that might collide.

IIISlice

Implement one coherent slice.

Format, lint, test, inspect the diff—in that order, every time. Weak tests and policy evasion get rejected, not merged.

IVCommit

Commit as punctuation.

A commit is a cognitive checkpoint between design and the next phase, not an end-of-day dump.

VAudit

Audit separately.

Correctness, security, performance, and documentation each get their own pass—and goal completion is judged apart from implementation completion.

The division of labor

Charter and audit (human)

Which problem, which invariants, what counts as done. Architecture and clean-break calls. Whether the evidence is sufficient. Refusal to ship when the audit fails—even when the agents are green.

Coordination and execution (agents)

Dispatch, sequencing, settlement. Reconnaissance, implementation, review passes, documentation.

In one fleet, 8,452 coordination events ran against 10 that required me. In a separate campaign, an audit caught specs naming tests that did not exist — a naive cargo test would have passed them. The system's job is to refuse, not just to execute.

The skill library

Make the model’s job easier.

Over the last six months, these repo-owned skills have grown into a cohesive layer of systems-engineering automation with one design goal: make the LLM’s job easier. Nearly every session on this page ran through them.

Multi-agent loops

/fleet

The newest, and a favorite: Mind, Head, and Hand roles for multi-agent project loops, with stewardship and dead-man continuation. Built on Vivi mailspaces, so agents coordinate over mail instead of shared memory.

Work-flow spine

/campaign · /factory · /delivery

The work-flow spine, used on practically everything. Campaigns and sub-campaigns organize the flow of work; a factory defines the phases for each unit; every phase gets a ledger that tracks it to completion.

Per-file loop

/polish

A per-file improvement loop backed by a deterministic analyzer that moves judgment calls out of the model and into repeatable checks. Polish is what keeps boilerplate LLM code from becoming slop.

Hygiene ratchets

/housekeeping

Repository hygiene with ratchets: not all code is perfect, so exceptions are explicit—and get ratcheted up or down per repo as the code earns it. This is how a codebase stays in clean, competent form.

Transcript evidence

/cassio

The skill behind the evidence on this page. Converts Claude, Codex, and OpenCode sessions into readable Markdown transcripts—and extracts training data for customized LLM tuning.

Agent mailspaces

/mail + Vivi

Arbitrary local mailspaces for agents and projects. Vivi grew out of the mail skill and became the substrate Fleet coordinates over—private email treated as durable agent infrastructure.

The backstory

A career in five acts.

Five acts, dated from executed agreements, payroll records, and public archives where those exist. The evidence ledger below says so when they don’t. Retirement appears once, late—not as the title of the page.

I1996—2009

Rifles, then a KDE front end for Nmap.

Four years in the Marine Corps—0351 Assaultman, out as a Corporal with an honorable discharge—left me with a bias toward responsibility that never wore off. In January 2000 I released KMap, a C++/Qt front end for Nmap. The Nmap 2.50 release notes mention it by name, which is still my favorite line of third-party evidence.

The path from there was not direct: five years selling real estate in Florida before joining Beatport’s API team in Denver in 2008, where electronic music distribution ran on Zend PHP, enterprise Java, Mule, Spring, Hibernate, Squid, Akamai, and MySQL.

USMC / 0351 / E-4C++ / Qt / KDEPHP / Java
II2009—2017

Salesforce, scale, and the founder turn.

At Red Hat I worked on a multilingual APAC Partner Center built on Salesforce—Korea, Japan, and China, twenty-odd sandboxes. roundCorner hired me in 2011 as Chief Scientist; I later held Head of Engineering for a Salesforce-native nonprofit CRM used by large institutions (including St. Jude, Georgetown, Duke). The product line was later acquired by Salesforce.org.

In 2015 I founded Minted.IO and, as a solo founder, built a metadata-driven multi-tenant application platform end to end in six months. After a year at Salesforce as a Technical Solution Architect—forward-deployed with CarMax and Home Depot—Motivis acquired the Minted.IO software/API assets in February 2017. My VP offer was written into the acquisition.

Chief Scientist → Head of EngFounder / acquiredForward-deployed TSA
III2017—2021

Platform leadership at scale.

At Motivis I led platform R&D across a team of roughly ten to twenty engineers in an approximately thirty-person company, on a TypeScript, Node.js, and PostgreSQL competency-based learning platform—with deployment portability across AWS, Google Cloud including Spanner, and local environments, and expansion from higher education into K-12.

From 2019 the Motivis relationship shifted toward independent platform and strategy consulting through Minted Geek entities. By 2021 I stepped away from conventional employment. That pause is Act IV; it is not the thesis of this site.

A short return, then a long pause.

In early 2021 I joined Kraken as Member of Technical Staff on money-movement systems (SWIFT and wire verification). I left after a few months. For about four years afterward I did not take conventional work. In August 2025 the tooling had changed enough that full-time building made sense again.

Full-time lab, measured output.

From August 2025 I returned to full-time independent R&D: Faber/Radix, Minted Geek Swarm, Monk, evaluation harnesses, and the orchestration layer described above. Commit volume and streak data are secondary evidence; the primary proof is falsifiable systems and instrumented process.
