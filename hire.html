What one operator produced.

Most engineering evidence is a logo — someone worked somewhere, and you infer the rest. What follows is measured instead: taken from live repositories and session logs, with the command that produced each figure written down. The repositories are open.

1,730 Agent-driven sessions in a single month that ran without me in the loop, up 12.7× since January. Session corpus
379,082 Tool calls in July — roughly fifteen machine actions for every instruction I typed. Session corpus
6.8× Throughput growth January to July while frontier-model usage fell from 80% of sessions to 1%. Volume up; the expensive tier gone. 125.9B tokens, 15,307 sessions
247,791 Lines deleted in one day replacing a TypeScript compiler with a Rust one — with no loss of test coverage, because the tests were never bound to the implementation. v0.24.0 → v0.25.0

That last one is the one I would actually want a founder to read. Deleting 83% of a codebase safely is not nerve; it is having built the thing so that it could be done. The full account of how — six parallel compiler implementations, a language-agnostic test corpus, and what it cost — is in the Radix retrospective.

What this does not show. No defect-escape-rate measurement — I know what the audit layer caught, not what it missed. No evidence the method transfers to a team; no other team has run it. And it holds where output is verifiable. Where failure is silent and delayed, or the real bottleneck is tacit institutional knowledge, the approach is weaker and I would say so before taking the engagement.

Product A

Fractional CTO.

Technical leadership for a smaller company — architecture, roadmap, hiring, and engineering process — delivered remote-first at a cadence that respects your runway and mine. Written charter. IP carve-out. Explicit exit path.

Who this is for

Pre-seed through Series A. Zero to fifteen engineers. Remote-first US. The pain that brings people here is usually one or two of:

  • Technical decisions are gating revenue, fundraising, or a launch.
  • Hiring without a senior bar, or unable to attract seniors without a leader in the loop.
  • Architecture or platform choices the current team cannot close.
  • Agent-generated code shipping without review, attribution, or cost control.
  • Bridge after a CTO left, or before the first full-time CTO hire.

Engagement shape

  • Weekly founder / CEO sync. Written 30 / 60 / 90 day outcomes.
  • Owned: architecture, roadmap alignment, hiring screens and debriefs, engineering process, critical-path review.
  • Not owned: ticket throughput, day-to-day project management, badge presence.
  • Optional onsite for launches, fundraises, or a key week per quarter — scoped separately.
  • Prior-inventions schedule and IP carve-out on every engagement.

Rates

Priced by the problem, not by the calendar. The range is wide on purpose — the same nominal engagement is a different job at a seed-stage company than at one with a board deadline and eleven services in production.

Focused
$10K – $25K
One bounded decision or system. An architecture read, a model-routing design, an audit of an agent pipeline before it scales.
Substantial · typical
$25K – $75K
A platform area taken from unclear to shipped. Several interacting decisions, hiring input, process that outlives me.
Sustained
$75K – $200K
Ongoing technical leadership across a roadmap. Quoted per quarter, renewed deliberately, with an explicit exit.

Where you land in the range is set by how tangled the problem is, how much is already on fire, and what a wrong answer costs you — not by how many hours it takes me. I will tell you which band I think you are in on the first call, before either of us has spent anything.

Good fit

  • Founder wants technical co-leadership, not a title
  • Remote-first cadence, weekly sync tolerated
  • Cash to fund at band for 6+ months
  • AI-native or platform product with real engineering pain

Not a fit

  • Onsite five days / week in SF
  • Equity-only in short runway conditions
  • Committee decision-maker with no single owner
  • Want to buy a productized methodology to install

Product B

Fractional Principal Engineer.

Senior hands-on systems work when the ask is capacity and judgment, not the CTO seat. Architecture and implementation on your codebase; embedded principal engineering; evaluation and audit-style work on shipping systems.

Who this is for

Engineering manager, founding engineer, or CTO who needs a specialist for a bounded stretch — usually one of:

  • Platform, runtime, compiler, or agent-fleet work outside the team's depth.
  • Architecture design + first implementation, handed off to a permanent owner.
  • Audit and evaluation on AI-generated code or model-routing infrastructure.
  • Short intense sprint on a critical path the team cannot free up for.

Rates

Scoped piece
$8K – $30K
A subsystem, a migration, a critical-path build with a definition of done we agree on up front.
Embedded
$30K – $90K
Sustained hands-on work across a quarter. Same output expectations, longer horizon.

No hourly, and no day rate. Both meter attention rather than outcome, and both quietly punish the thing that makes this worth buying. I run fleets of agents in parallel; work that used to take a team a quarter can take days. If you are paying for my calendar you are buying the wrong unit — and I would be pricing the work at a fraction of what it is worth to you. So the number is attached to the result.

For recruiters

Full-time engagements.

Full-time is a capstone, not the opening move. If a role is genuinely staff / principal / architect IC or a small-company VP-of-Engineering / CTO seat, it is worth a conversation. Director-plus roles with heavy people-management-only accountability are usually not the right fit.

Filter before you send

  • Remote-first US. Will not relocate to SF. Austin considered case by case.
  • Small engineering org. ~30 engineers or fewer where the technical judgment matters.
  • Staff / principal / architect IC, or small-company VP Eng / CTO. Not layered director seats.
  • Product with a verifiable correctness surface. Compilers, runtimes, platforms, agent systems, developer tools.
  • Cash + equity in that order. Equity-heavy early roles by exception, not default.
