Fractional

Fractional CTO.

Technical leadership for a smaller company: architecture, roadmap, hiring, and engineering process, delivered remote-first. Written charter. IP carve-out. Explicit exit path. No published rate card; the number comes from a first call about the problem.

Who this is for

Pre-seed through Series A. Zero to fifteen engineers. Remote-first US. Common reasons people write:

  • Technical decisions are gating revenue, fundraising, or a launch.
  • Hiring without a senior bar, or unable to attract seniors without a leader in the loop.
  • Architecture or platform choices the current team cannot close.
  • Agent-generated code shipping without review, attribution, or cost control.
  • Bridge after a CTO left, or before the first full-time CTO hire.

Engagement shape

  • Weekly founder / CEO sync. Written 30 / 60 / 90 day outcomes.
  • Owned: architecture, roadmap alignment, hiring screens and debriefs, engineering process, critical-path review.
  • Not owned: ticket throughput, day-to-day project management, badge presence.
  • Onsite for launches, fundraises, board meetings, or a key week per quarter, scoped separately.
  • Prior-inventions schedule and IP carve-out on every engagement.

On being in the room

Remote-first is the default cadence. Onsite weeks are available when the work needs them. At Salesforce I was a forward-deployed technical solution architect embedded with CarMax and Home Depot. At roundCorner I was Vice President of Software Engineering, promoted from Chief Scientist, with no CTO above me. I was a founder through an acquisition.

What made those work was having enough technical depth to understand the complexity, and enough vocabulary to explain it to a product owner or an executive team without flattening it into something false. Problems usually arrive as trade-offs, not binary choices. The useful contribution is making the trade legible.

Engagement sizes

Focused
One bounded decision or system. An architecture read, a model-routing design, an audit of an agent pipeline before it scales.
Substantial · typical
A platform area taken from unclear to shipped. Several interacting decisions, hiring input, process that outlives the engagement.
Sustained
Ongoing technical leadership across a roadmap. Quoted per quarter, renewed deliberately, with an explicit exit.

How the number is set. How tangled the problem is, how much is already on fire, and what a wrong answer costs you, not how many hours it takes. You will hear a number on the first call, before either side has spent anything.

Good fit

  • Founder wants technical co-leadership, not a title
  • Remote-first cadence, weekly sync tolerated
  • Cash runway to fund 6+ months
  • AI-native or platform product with real engineering pain

Not a fit

  • Onsite five days / week in SF
  • Equity-only in short runway conditions
  • Committee decision-maker with no single owner
  • Want to buy a productized methodology to install

Hands-on

Fractional Principal Engineer.

Senior hands-on systems work when the ask is capacity and judgment, not the CTO seat. Architecture and implementation on your codebase; embedded principal engineering; evaluation and audit-style work on shipping systems. Priced by the piece and by the result, not by the clock.

Who this is for

Engineering manager, founding engineer, or CTO who needs a specialist for a bounded stretch, usually one of:

  • Platform, runtime, compiler, or agent-fleet work outside the team’s depth.
  • Architecture design plus first implementation, handed off to a permanent owner.
  • Audit and evaluation on AI-generated code or model-routing infrastructure.
  • Short intense sprint on a critical path the team cannot free up for.

Engagement sizes

Scoped piece
A subsystem, a migration, or a critical-path build with a definition of done agreed up front.
Embedded
Sustained hands-on work across a quarter. Same output expectations, longer horizon.

No hourly rate, and no day rate. Both meter attention rather than outcome. Work that used to take a team a quarter can take days when agent fleets run in parallel. The number is attached to the result.
